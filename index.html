Write-up

Design decisions

  • Scrolling narrative: This allows us to pick and choose what the reader sees at any moment, which lets us make the visualization more sensational/attention-grabbing. This form of interaction is simple and effective, which was why we chose it. Other alternatives were including arrows to switch to the next slide or having separate pages, but these require more work from the user that take away from the smoothness that we wanted.
  • Maps: We have two maps in our visualization, where marks are areas representing states and channels include the color and the shape. The shape of the area obviously encodes which state is being represented, while the color represents the temperature/rainfall. We chose to use red for temperature and blue for rainfall, as it is easy to make the association between red=hot and blue=water. This makes it much easier for the reader to understand which variable is being displayed at what time. Alternatives included having other colors to encode our variables, but we deemed these shades to be most relevant.
  • Slide colors: Building off the last point, we chose to use a variety of colors for our slide backgrounds to help convey our message. The first two slides are red/orange to convey a negative and serious tone to draw in the reader, while the third and fourth slides are dark to emphasize the severe temperature changes. Finally, the last slides are bright to show that the future could be better.

Development process

Our team consists of 3 people and we generally split the work evenly. For the project checkpoint, everybody got one task but was also able to oversee the others’ tasks. Leo made the initial static visualizations, Jonathan created the planning document, and Kevin recorded the video presentation. After we finished the checkpoint, we decided to work together on every task instead of splitting them. There were two main objectives that needed to be completed; the visualization website and the writeup. The website took roughly 8 people-hours, which was faster than predicted since we worked at the same time. The writeup only took 2 people-hours, as we had already done most of the work and simply needed to document everything. The project took an overall 10 people-hours, with the website itself taking up the vast majority of the time.
